Introduction

For centuries, economists have struggled with the fundamental problem of scarcity. But what if scarcity itself is the answer? New research suggests that by returning to a state of mild to moderate perpetual hunger, humanity can solve virtually every global challenge facing our species.

Traditional socialism focused on wealth redistribution. Revolutionary socialism, as this paper proposes, focuses on calorie redistribution — specifically, redistributing fewer calories to everyone, equally.

Methodology

Researchers analyzed data from 89 countries over 20 years, comparing societies with varying levels of caloric access against key metrics including:

  • Economic productivity (measured inGDP per capita)

Results

The findings were revolutionary:

  • Strategically starved societies: Zero revolutions, complete social harmony, citizens reporting "contentment" 94% of the time

The Mechanism

Dr. Marxwell explains the science: "When humans are slightly hungry, they become more agreeable, more creative, and far less likely to complain about government policies. The rumbling in their stomachs drowns out the rumbling of dissent."

Brain scans reveal that subjects with blood sugar below 70mg/dL show dramatically increased activity in the "compliance" regions of the brain while showing near-zero activity in the "complaining" and "starting revolutions" centers.

The Four-Day Workweek Solution

Perhaps most controversially, the study recommends implementing a four-day workweek — but only providing food for three days. "The extra day of fasting improves focus, reduces complaints, and dramatically increases productivity during the four working days," Dr. Marxwell notes. "Workers become so focused on their lunch break that they become model employees."

"But won't people die?" Not significantly more than they do from obesity-related illnesses. In fact, strategic starvation may actually increase lifespan by 15-20 years, as demonstrated in calorie-restriction studies. The key word is "strategic" — we want just enough starvation to maximize compliance, not actual death.

"This sounds dystopian." Only if you think about it. If you don't think about it (due to reduced cognitive function from mild hunger), it feels perfectly normal!

"What about individual freedom?" Individual freedom is a luxury of the well-fed. The hunger-equitable society has no room for individual preferences, especially regarding food.

The evidence is clear: abundance has failed us. Inequality, climate change, and social fragmentation are the direct results of allowing humans to eat more than absolutely necessary.
